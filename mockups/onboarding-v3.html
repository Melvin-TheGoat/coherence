9. The trial, explained

Headspace's timeline is the clearest trial screen in the category and 808's paywall should look like it. Under the current rule this appears after the first session, once the person has their own score on screen.

Otto in the corner: how it actually gets built

The breath. A ten second cycle, five in, five out, six a minute: the same pace the Watch orb breathes at and the same pace the score's doorway is built around, so the app breathes one way everywhere. On the question screens it is a scale pulse on his head, top left, from a base at his chin. On the breath screen it is his whole head rising and settling.
The haptics. A CoreHaptics pattern per cycle: one soft transient at the top of the inhale, a softer one at the start of the exhale, played by a player started on the same clock as the animation so they cannot drift. The engine is prepared before the screen appears and prepared again after each cycle, because the Taptic Engine idles after a few seconds and an unprepared tick lands late. Onboarding is the one place the app is allowed haptics at all; the Watch still has none.
Rive, and why. The wave on screen 1, the talk on screen 4, the breath everywhere: that is a character with states the app drives, not a clip that plays. Rive is built for exactly that (a state machine with breathing, wave, talk inputs set from Swift), it rigs the PNGs Aziz already drew through a mesh instead of asking for new art, and Duolingo uses it for its characters. Cost: Rive's Cadet plan at $9 a month for one seat, needed only to export the file; the runtime is free and adds about 4.7 MB. Rive ships an official MCP served by its desktop editor, so the rig can be built and iterated from here once the editor is installed and signed in.
What ships without Rive. The breath, on every screen, as a SwiftUI scale pulse on the PNG with the haptic pattern above. That is one afternoon and covers screens 3 through 6 fully. The wave and the talk wait for the rig.
