D. Otto's clearing

Character first. The top half is a place: a bough comes in from the corner, Otto sits under it, the greeting shares the sky with him. Everything measured lives below the streak and you scroll for it. Closest to Finch, the one mascot app in this category that worked.

Trade: the week is the only card above the fold. Right if Home is a greeting; wrong if Home is a dashboard. That question decides between B and D.

How Otto moves, in every one of these

Three clips, one rig. Idle is a breath: a slow scale from the base, six a minute, matching the Watch orb, so the two pace the same thing. Wave plays once when Home opens. Talk plays while his bubble is on screen (mouth only; the body keeps breathing). A tap on him replays talk with a fresh line.
Rive, not Lottie, not hand-coded. Duolingo animates its characters with Rive (their engineering blog, November 2022). Rive rigs the existing PNG through a mesh with bones, so Aziz's drawings are reused rather than redrawn, and the app drives it as a state machine: breathing on and off, wave and talk as triggers, the inhale sent from Swift so the haptic fires on the same clock. It costs $9 a month for one seat to export and about 4.7 MB of app size. Rive publishes an official MCP served by its desktop editor, so the rig can be built from here. The fallback that ships this week with zero dependencies is a SwiftUI scale pulse on the PNG: a good breath, no wave, no talk.
What to decide. Is Home a greeting or a dashboard? B keeps it a dashboard with a friend in the corner. D makes it his. A and C are in between and both need one new drawing. Pick one and the next mockup is that one at full fidelity with real copy for every hour of the day.
