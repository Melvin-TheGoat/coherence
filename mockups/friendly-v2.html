808, built around Otto

The character is soft, dusty and low contrast, so a bright app would fight him. Instead every colour in the interface is lifted out of the drawing: the paper is his background, the ink is his eyes, the streak is his cheeks. Nothing in the app is allowed a colour he does not already have.

The art here is cut out, not pasted on. The generated PNGs came with his cream background baked in, which would have put a faint square around him on every screen. Each pose is now separated from that background with a soft alpha edge and trimmed to his own outline, so he sits on paper, on a white card and on a tinted one without a seam. That is also the first thing to specify when the real art is commissioned: transparency, not a background that happens to match.

What changes in the code

  1. Seven colorsets get new values and two are added (blush for the streak, paper for the ground). Every colour in the app already routes through AppColor, so this is a data change, not a refactor.
  2. AppFont switches to design: .rounded. One line, and it does more for friendliness than anything else here.
  3. Light becomes the default theme. Dark stays and takes the same warm treatment: paper goes to a deep cocoa rather than near-black, and Otto needs a dark-mode variant from the illustrator.
  4. DesignKit: radii 12 to 26, hairlines become soft bottom edges, buttons gain the pressed edge, calendar and score chips become filled rather than outlined.
  5. OttoArt.swift is deleted. The path-parsing approach was right for a one-colour glyph and is wrong for this: a coloured character ships as an asset. Xcode asset catalogs take SVG as a single-scale vector with light and dark variants, so the illustrator's file goes in untouched and SVGPath, OttoMark, OttoSlothSitting and their tests all go away.
  6. Nothing about the charts' honesty changes. Same domains, same fixed spans, same doorway band. Thicker rounded strokes, that is all.

What I am deliberately not touching: the copy. This is a skin. If the words move at the same time as the paint, there is no way to tell which one did the work.

What is still owed on the art. These are 1254px generated rasters cut down to between 290 and 970 pixels a side, which is enough for a mockup and not enough to ship: the 186pt Begin sheet and the 200pt award screen would both be soft on a 3x phone. The brief for an illustrator is these images as the reference, and the deliverable is vector, on transparency, with a dark-mode variant, clean at the five sizes he appears: 46pt avatar, 52pt chat, 78pt streak card, 186pt sheet, 200pt award.
