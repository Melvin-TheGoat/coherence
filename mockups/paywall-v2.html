Pricing and the paywall · Oct 26, 2026

Keep monthly, lead with yearly, and turn the half-off into a banner

Read against RevenueCat's State of Subscription Apps 2026 and our own 26 days on the premium paywall. The short version: going yearly-only would cut the plan most of our buyers pick, and $7.99 is already under the Health & Fitness median, so the place to give ground on price is the banner, aimed at the people who hesitate.

No

Yearly only

Single-plan paywalls are the rarest kind in Health & Fitness (about 20%); two plans is the norm (60%). Five of our seven full-price buyers chose Monthly.

Change

Preselect Yearly and show it as $2.50 a month

This gets Aziz the yearly push without removing monthly. Yearly-led apps earn about 60% more per install by day 60 ($0.46 against $0.29). Today Monthly is the preselected card, which is part of why it wins.

Keep

Monthly at $7.99 for now

The category median is $9.99, so we are already cheap. At $4.99 we would need 60% more monthly buyers just to stay even, and Yearly's saving would shrink from 69% to 50%. The banner gives $3.99 to the people who balk, without cutting everyone else's price.

Change

Half-off becomes one banner, one tap to buy

It slides up on the paywall when someone taps "No, I don't want to pay" or backs out of Apple's purchase sheet. That second trigger matters: 13 people opened Apple's sheet for Monthly and cancelled.

Change

Make the free trial 7 days, not 3

3-day trials see 55% cancel on day zero against 40% for 7-day ones, and convert 25.5% against 37.4%. It is a setting in App Store Connect; the app already reads the length from there.

Suggest

Take Lifetime off the paywall

22 people tapped the card, one opened Apple's sheet, nobody bought. Lifetime earns the least per install of any plan in the report. The product stays for restores and anyone who already owns it. This one is your call; you brought it back on Sep 29.

Same valley, same panel. The paywall loses a card and leads with Yearly. The banner replaces the two full-screen rungs you have to tap through now.

How the banner behaves

  1. Two triggers. Tapping "No, I don't want to pay", or cancelling Apple's purchase sheet for any plan. Once per paywall visit.
  2. One tap to buy. "Claim half price" opens Apple's sheet for the half-price plan directly. Today a rung only selects the plan and sends you back to the paywall to press Continue again.
  3. It replaces both rungs. The half-price plan already starts with the free trial, so one banner covers what the trial rung and the half-off rung did. The trial rung got 12 accepts and 3 full-price trials in 26 days, so little is lost.
  4. No thanks or the ✕ lowers it and leaves the paywall as it was, with "No, I don't want to pay" gone.
  5. What App Review needs on it: the billed price as the biggest number, the real "was" price ($7.99 is what Monthly charges), the renewal terms, and Terms and Privacy links, since it sells on its own. No countdown timer: fake urgency reads as misleading, and it would not fit how 808 talks.

Across all apps:

  • Plans: yearly-led apps earn $0.46 per install by day 60, monthly-led $0.29, lifetime-led $0.24.
  • Price: higher-priced apps convert better (2.8% against 1.4%) and earn far more per install ($0.94 against $0.11 by day 60). This is a correlation, but it runs against cutting price to win buyers.
  • Showing yearly as a monthly figure: one contributor (Mojo) saw trial starts rise 30% and yearly take rate rise 10% from it.
  • Trials: 3-day trials lose 55.4% on day zero, 7-day 39.8%. Trial-to-paid is 25.5% for 4 days or less and 37.4% for 5 to 9.
  • Timing: about half of all purchases happen on the day of install, which is why the moment someone hesitates on the paywall is worth a real offer.
  • Discounts: every discounted dollar lowers lifetime value, so a discount should go to people who would not pay full price, not to everyone. That is the case for a banner over a lower list price.

Not on the public page: retention and renewal by plan length, and refunds. Those are in the 330-page PDF. If you send it, I'll add how yearly and monthly subscribers hold up over 12 months, which is the other half of the yearly-only question.

If you still want $4.99

Ship the banner and the Yearly preselect first, give it four weeks, and look at how many people take the banner. If most hesitators take $3.99, the list price is the problem and $4.99 or $5.99 is worth a test. If few take it, price was never the reason they left. Changing several things at once with 100 people a month would leave us unable to tell what worked.
