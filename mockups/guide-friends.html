Guide and Friends

The last two screens still speaking the old language. Guide is a vertical rail of hollow circles with outlined level chips; Friends buries four stats in the middle of a post and hangs the photo off the bottom. Both get the grammar everything else now uses: filled tokens, aligned columns, one amber thing.

The rail is gone and the level became a heading. A teal line with hollow circles said "these are a progression" in the one visual language the app has otherwise dropped, and it said it about a list nobody has to take in order. Three headings say the same thing in words and cost no ink.

The token is the point. Each technique carries how many times YOU have sat it: amber and raised if you have, an empty hollow if you have not. Same object as an earned award, same object as a day on the week strip. The Guide stops being a menu and becomes a shelf of what you have tried.

The photo moves up and the stats move down. Four stats sat in the middle of the card with the photo dangling off the bottom, so the picture (the reason anybody stops scrolling) was last and the numbers interrupted the sentence. Now: who, what they called it, what it looked like, then the numbers on a hairline, then the reaction.

The score rides on the photo, as a capsule, exactly as it does on a session card in the app. That takes it out of the stat row, leaves three columns that line up card to card, and keeps one amber object per post.

Requests become a button. "1 request" was grey text floating above a search field and was the single most missable thing in the product: it is the only screen where somebody is waiting on you.
