The session screen, on the branch

Otto sits where he already lives, and the sky carries the time so no number has to. It opens on a blue morning, the light goes as you settle, it is night in the middle of the sit, and the day comes back when he opens his eyes. The blues are picked against the leaf green that is already in the art, so it reads as the same world rather than a different app.

Morning. One instruction, once: put it down. That is the sentence that makes the foreground rule feel like care rather than surveillance.

0:20  the light goes
End

The sky turns itself down over about twenty seconds. We deleted dark mode and this is the one place it was load-bearing: a bright screen at 10pm is a lamp in your face. It reads as dusk falling rather than a setting.

4:12  you tapped
5:48
End

No number unless you ask. A countdown is a thing to watch and watching it is the opposite of the exercise. The ring is there the whole time; a tap shows the figure for three seconds.

10:00  he opens his eyes

Otto’s fine for today.
Instagram is open again
Done

The day comes back and Otto opens his eyes, the only animation in the app that has to land. The shield lifting is said out loud, because that is what the ten minutes bought.

Why blue is allowed here and nowhere else. The app is cream and amber and that does not change. This screen is scenery rather than interface: it is the one place the person is looking at a place instead of a product, and the sky is how the time is told. The blues were chosen against the leaf green already in the art, so it is Otto's world at a different hour, not a second theme.

What the sky replaces. A progress bar, a countdown, and the pacer orb. Three pieces of interface become one thing that is also nice to look at, and none of them can be watched anxiously, which is the entire point.
