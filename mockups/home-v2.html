Otto is the app now

Aziz: Otto replaces the flower and becomes the main thing. The reference is Finch, which is the only proof in this category that it works: 754,000 ratings, 4.9, Editors' Choice, and the bird is enormous in every single screenshot. The pattern underneath it is worth naming, because it is not "put a mascot on the screen": the mascot sits somewhere. Finch's bird stands on grass under a sky. He is in a place, and the interface floats around him. A character on a flat card is a sticker; a character on a horizon is somebody's morning.

The other half of the research was a negative and it is worth having. Every well-regarded meditation home screen on Dribbble is dark, glassy and photographic: black grounds, mountains, glowing rings, 82/100 in a thin circle. That is a real and popular look and it is exactly the one 808 already had and you are moving away from. Nothing on that page is inspiration for this.

Four things, top to bottom, and nothing else. Who you are and when it is. Otto. What your streak is. What you have done. The screen you have today has a logo, a greeting, a streak block, a sparkline, a calendar, a list and a debug line, which is seven, and none of them is the reason you opened the app.

Why the scene, and not a card

The sky is doing real work. A single soft gradient from peach to paper, with a curved bottom edge, turns the top third of the screen into a place rather than a panel. It is four lines of CSS here and one gradient in Swift, and it is the whole difference between Otto being in the app and Otto being on it. The faint horizon line under him is what stops him floating.

The streak leaves its box

It is a white capsule straddling the edge of the scene, the way a price tag sits on a card. That does two things: it physically joins the scene to the content below it, and it lets the streak be small. A 54pt number was the loudest thing on Home and it is not the point of the app; Otto is. Best and total sits ride along behind a divider instead of stacking in a corner.

The calendar becomes round

Circles, not squares, and no card inside a card. A practised day is a solid amber disc with the date in it, today is a sage outline. Five weeks, not six: the row of greyed next-month days at the bottom was pure filler. This is the object people will look at most, since it is the only place the app shows you a month of your own life, so it gets the most air on the screen.

The sparkline is gone

It drew seven scores at 46 points tall next to a calendar that draws thirty days, and the two were telling the same story at different resolutions. The calendar wins: it is the one people understand without a label. The score history still lives on Profile, where somebody has gone looking for it.

The app icon

Finch's icon is the bird's face cropped so tight it bleeds off the tile, and it is why you can find it on a home screen of two hundred apps. Otto's head, eyes open, filling the corners, on his own sky. The flower goes.

What replaces the flower, everywhere it appears

The mark is in more places than the home screen, so this is a list rather than a gesture: the app icon, the launch screen, the top of Home, the share card's corner, the onboarding screens, and the Watch's start screen. Otto takes the first three. The share card and the Watch keep a mark, because a 38pt sloth head on a 1080-wide card and a 24pt one on a 40mm watch both turn to mud, and that is the one job line art is better at. So the flower does not die, it becomes the small-print signature, and Otto becomes the face.
