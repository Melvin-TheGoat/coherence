Zen mode and Monk mode

Melvin's decision, 2026-11-06. Zen mode is the default: lock the phone, put the screen to sleep, even open another app, and the timer keeps going. Monk mode is opt-in and is roughly today's rules. Frames are 390 wide (iPhone 17 Pro), drawn in the app's valley, sand cards, cream pills, green for doing and sky blue for choosing. Design only, nothing built.

The card a person sees when they open the choice (Settings, or the chip on the + screen). Same words in both places. The rest day is the same in both, so there is one streak rule, not two.

Copy notes. Monk's "recorded by hand" cell says what happens ("Saved to your history"), not a cross, so the table never tells somebody what their session is missing. The "Ends after 10 seconds" cells are the only terracotta on the card: it is the one real cost.

Naming. Zen reads as calm, not as "easy", so the default is never the lesser way to meditate. Monk matches Otto's Monk's hat in the Shop, which could be the badge for the mode later.

Where Zen is looser than the CLAUDE.md write-up: that draft kept "another app ends it after 10 seconds" in the default. Melvin's decision lets Zen switch apps too. That moves the Block question in the open questions below from a footnote to a real decision.

3. The + screen

Aziz has taken switches off this screen before for clutter, so the mode is never a switch here. Two options: a small chip under the clock that opens the choice as a sheet, or nothing at all and the mode lives in Settings only.

3a  a quiet chip

"Zen mode ›" sits under the timer tape, the size of a caption. It is the only new thing on the screen and it is not a control you have to read past to Begin.

3a  tapping the chip

The same two rows as Settings in a sheet. Picking one closes it. Choosing here changes the setting, not just this session, so there is only one place the mode is stored.

3b  no chip ALSO FINE

Today's screen, unchanged. Most people never change the default, and the sit screen itself says which mode is running (section 4). Monk users set it once in Settings.

4. The live session, in each mode

The same dusk valley and medallion clock as today. One line under the headline names the mode's rule, and it fades after the first half minute like the opening frame does today.

4a  Zen mode

"Lock your phone if you like. I'll keep time." (44 characters). This replaces today's "Keep 808 open to ensure you are meditating", which is the very rule Zen removes.

4b  Monk mode

"Monk mode. Keep 808 open until the bell." (40 characters). Under it a small pill says the rule once: "Leaving for 10 seconds ends the session." Same words as the table.

5. Coming back after locking the phone

Zen: the session simply carries on, and Otto says so once. Monk: the session has ended, and the screen offers to start again. There is no "I was still meditating" in Monk mode.

5a  Zen, unlocking mid-session

Nothing to tap. The clock reads the real elapsed time (12:40 in, of 20), Otto's bubble shows for a few seconds and fades. If the timer ran out while locked, the Time Sensitive notification already rang and this opens straight on the reward.

5b  Monk, returning after 10 seconds

The session up to the moment you left is not kept, as today. The line reports what happened in plain words and points at the fix ("Start again"), and the smaller link tells the person Zen exists without arguing for it.

6. Otto offers Monk mode, once, after a 7-day streak

On a quiet Home after the reward screen of the seventh day in a row, in the same slot as the rating ask (never the same day as it). Shown once per phone. "Not now" never comes back.

6  the offer

"Seven days in a row! Want to try Monk mode?" (43 characters). Framed as a step up the person earned, not a correction. "Stay in Zen" is a cream pill of equal size, so saying no is as easy as saying yes.

Why after seven days. Commitment devices help the people who choose them, and few do (11% take-up in Gine, Karlan and Zinman 2010). A week in a row is the first moment someone has shown they want more structure, and offering earlier would put a strict mode in front of somebody still deciding whether to come back tomorrow.

Tour line (onboarding's last tour stop, 74 characters max):
"Lock your phone if you like. I'll keep time. Monk mode is in Settings." (70)

Analytics: the switch (zen / monk, and where it was changed), and whether the offer was taken. A choice only, nothing biometric.

Open questions

  1. An open-ended session with the phone locked has no end. Points are one per whole minute, so a session left running overnight would pay out hours. Options: cap open-ended Zen sessions (say 2 hours, then it ends itself at the cap), or ask on return when it has run past a limit ("Still meditating? You've been going 3 hours") with the minutes kept only up to when the phone was last touched. Recommended: the cap, because it needs no answer from someone who has already left.
  2. Should a Zen session spent in other apps still open Block's apps? One minute of session opens them for the day, so a "session" spent scrolling TikTok would unlock TikTok.
    A. Time in other apps doesn't count toward Block's minuteLocked-screen time counts, app-switch time doesn't. The session still counts for streak, glow and points. Recommended, if a lock can be told from a switch (question 3).
    B. Any Zen session still opens BlockSimplest and honest to the "your way" promise, but it makes Zen the way round Block for anyone who wants one. If chosen, Block users may want Monk as their default.
  3. Telling "locked" from "switched apps" needs a phone test. Apple's protectedDataWillBecomeUnavailable is the documented signal for a lock, but its timing, whether it fires with no passcode set, and whether a silent suspended app receives it are unverified. The simulator cannot answer this. If it fails, option A in question 2 is not buildable and the choice falls to B or to Monk-for-Block.
  4. A silent session can be killed by iOS while locked. The active session only lives in memory today, so Zen needs it saved at Begin and finished on the next launch. Sessions with sound stay alive through the audio background mode.
  5. Monk mode and recorded sessions. Stamp the rule on each session when it is written, so switching modes later never rewrites past streaks.
