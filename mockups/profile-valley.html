Profile, second pass

You were right that something was off, and the answer was already written down in this repo. The fourteen-app research behind Save session says: no boxes, bare text on a hairline, full bleed between sections, section headers bold sentence case, one filled object. The last mockup was four floating boxes in a stack.

The rule I had been applying wrongly. A cream pill floating on the valley is a control you press: Sound, Silence notifications, a track, Begin. Profile is almost entirely content you read, and four identical floating boxes turn reading matter into a row of buttons, which is exactly the "five slabs" the research rejected. Same app, two different jobs, two different objects.

So: one page, not four boxes. The valley is a band you can actually see, with Otto sitting in it, and your practice is a single sheet resting on it with hairlines between the sections. It also fixes the other thing that was wrong: the last version buried the scene behind content from the first pixel, so the "valley style" was a strip of sky.

The four decisions

Sessions are rows here and cards on Home, deliberately. A card inside a sheet is a box inside a box, which is the thing being fixed. Strava does the same: cards in the feed, rows on the profile. The content is identical, only the container differs.

The shelf keeps its squircles because an award IS an object, and the one place a filled shape belongs on a page of text is the thing meant to be collected. That is the page's one filled object, and there is no other, since Profile has no primary action.

Blush for the stats, amber for the scores. The streak is blush everywhere in 808 and amber means a measured number, so the four counters and the bars are deliberately different colours rather than a wall of gold.

Otto keeps his line but loses the tail. A speech bubble pointing at him across a band he is centred in would cross the scene; the line reads as his because he is the only one there. He is the meditating pose, sitting in your valley, not the greeting one waving you somewhere.
