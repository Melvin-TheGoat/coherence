The discipline underneath all of it: orange appears on exactly four things (Cancel, the dashed add-photo tile, one outlined button, and the save button) and only one of them is filled. Everything else on the screen is neutral. That restraint is what makes it read as clean rather than as a form.

One deliberate departure: Cancel stays neutral grey here, where Strava paints it orange. 808's rule is one gold thing per section, and in that header it has to be the score. Gold therefore lands three times on the whole screen, once per section: the score, the add-selfie tile, the save button.

Worth knowing, from the research

  1. SweatMates forces the dual front-and-back shot, and it is their most-complained-about feature. Real App Store reviews object to being made to use the back camera, one specifically about capturing bystanders at the gym. Since you named SweatMates as a reference, that is the thing I would not copy from it. One front camera, as agreed.
  2. BeReal puts the caption AFTER posting, not before. Our description sits on the save screen, which is Strava's model, and I think that is right for us because the save screen is also where the session gets graded.
  3. Strava's current build dropped its stats strip and demoted the map to a thumbnail. We keep three numbers, because unlike a run, a meditation has nothing else to show and the score is the reason this screen exists.

Two more reference passes across Hevy, Gentler Streak, Nike Run Club, Whoop, Letterboxd and the meditation apps are still running. If they turn up something that makes this cleaner I will fold it in before writing any Swift.
