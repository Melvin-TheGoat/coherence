Ready when you are

One scene, edge to edge. Otto is not in a panel at the top of a settings page, he is the screen, sitting in the daylight version of the valley you are about to be in. The controls float on the meadow in front of him, so tapping Begin does not change screens: the pills clear, the ring fades up, and the sun starts moving.

The notifications row, before you judge it. iOS gives apps no way to turn on Do Not Disturb. No API, and the private App-prefs: deep links are a rejection, so a switch that silently does it cannot be built by anyone. What can be built is on panel 2: a Shortcuts automation the user makes once, which is genuinely automatic afterwards. The switch is drawn as a switch because after that setup it behaves like one.

Let's make this automatic

Apple doesn't let 808 switch on Do Not Disturb by itself. It lets you switch it on for 808, once, and then it happens every time.

1
Open Shortcuts, then Automation.
2
New automation → App → pick 808 → Is Opened.
3
Add Set Focus → Do Not Disturb → On. Run immediately.
4
Repeat for Is Closed, with Focus Off, so your phone comes back.
Open Shortcuts
I'll do it later

It triggers on the app opening, not on Begin. Apple's app automations have no finer trigger, and it is close enough: opening 808 is something you do to meditate. The cost is that checking your history at lunch also silences you until you leave, which the closing half undoes.

The switch reads the phone, it does not remember a preference. 808 can ask iOS whether a Focus is on right now (one permission, no entitlement), so the pill states what is true of the phone in your hand. A pill that says "off" while a banner slides over Otto is worse than no pill.

The one decision I need from you

Is the Shortcuts route worth it? It is the only thing that actually silences a phone, it works, and it costs a one-time setup screen plus a trip out to Shortcuts that some people will abandon. The alternative is cutting the pill and keeping the one-line Control Center tip that is on the screen today. I would build it: "nothing will interrupt" is half of what this screen promises, and a tip is not a promise. But the pill must never sit there looking on when nothing is silenced, which is why panel 1 is the un-set-up state.
