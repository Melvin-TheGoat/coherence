The sit, in a valley

Composition only. The mountains and field here are blocked in with flat shapes so the geometry can be judged before the real art exists: where the horizon falls, how big the sun is, how large Otto sits in the frame. Those are the numbers that are painful to change later. The sun sets across the ten minutes, which is the arc I would argue for over my earlier one.

He sits on a cushion, in a meadow. The seat gives him somewhere to be rather than floating on grass, and it is the one warm object in a cool frame. Flowers are scattered by depth: bigger and brighter near the bottom edge, smaller and paler toward the ridge, which is what makes a flat field read as ground going away from you. The clock is here and only here. At 0:00 nobody is meditating yet, they are arriving, and knowing it is ten minutes before you commit is reassuring rather than something to watch. It fades with the rest of the text once the sit starts. Horizon at 34 percent, Otto 186pt, sun 44pt just above the ridge: the dashed lines mark the three numbers that are painful to change later.

The sun does the work a progress bar would. It is the one thing on screen that is honestly moving, and it cannot be read precisely, which is exactly what you want somebody looking at mid-sit.

It ends at dusk rather than back in daylight. A sunrise would be a brighter screen at the exact moment somebody is most settled, and the quiet afterglow is a better reward than a bright one.

What is real here and what is placeholder. Real: the composition numbers, the sun's path, the darkening curve, where the ring and the number sit. Placeholder: the mountains and the field, which are flat polygons and will look wrong beside a painted sloth. Those become one generated landscape in Otto's own style.

What is code and what is art, once the picture exists. The sky is a gradient interpolated by elapsed time, so it moves continuously and costs nothing. The sun is a disc and a glow, also code, because it has to travel. The landscape is a PNG with a transparent sky above the ridge line, sitting between the two. Otto is the Rive rig he already is. Nothing here needs a new tool.

The branch is still available. Swapping the field for the limb is one layer; if he should be up a tree looking out over the valley rather than sitting in it, say so and it is a five-minute change while everything else holds.
