808 · Otto is a sloth

Otto, the on-device chat that reads your sessions back to you, gets a mascot: a sloth. Slow, peaceful, on brand for an app whose whole promise is settling down. These are concepts to react to, not final art. Everything here is line art in the language of the 808 mark: one stroke weight, round caps, no fills except a soft wash for the sloth's eye mask, no photo, no emoji. The sloth's eyes are closed in every drawing. That is the one decision the four boards share, because a sloth with its eyes shut is already meditating.

Four artboards

1 · Sitting. Cross-legged, eyes closed, hands resting on the knees with the three claws draped over each one. The right foot tucks out under the left knee. The pose a user recognises as meditating, and the drawing Otto's locked screen and empty state would carry.
2 · Hanging. The classic silhouette: belly to the branch, back sagging like a hammock, four limbs hooked over with three claws each, head at the end, asleep. Arms curl forward, legs curl back. For a splash, a loading state, or the paid-tier store screenshot.

3 · Head badge. The circle is the head, the same ring the placeholder "O" uses today. Stroke is 5 percent of the diameter, which is the weight the existing ring already has at 24 and 32 pt (1.2 and 1.6 px). The same paths at 160, 56, 32 and 24 px, nothing redrawn.
4 · Beside the mark. The 808 mark at 138 pt, gold, with its own stroke ratio from LogoMark.swift (0.026 of the size, so 3.6 here). The sloth and the badge in the same 3.6 stroke, teal. Same weight, same round caps, same ellipses doing the work. They should read as one hand.

Colour

Teal. In this app gold means chosen or achieved and teal means the body's signals and guidance. Otto is guidance: it explains, it never awards. A gold sloth would also be a second gold object on every screen it sits on (the results ring, the send button, the unlock button already own that colour), and the rule is one gold thing per section. The Otto mockup already draws its placeholder ring in teal for the same reason; the sloth simply moves into that ring. White is shown for completeness. It works, but it would be the only white line drawing in the app and reads as a system glyph rather than a character. Gold is there so the founders can see it, and it is the right answer only if Otto is ever repositioned as a reward rather than a guide.

Decisions in this mockup

  • Eyes closed everywhere. One face, three drawings. Closed eyes are calm at 240 pt and legible at 24, and they make the badge a meditator rather than a pet.
  • The mask is the only wash. The three-toed sloth's dark eye stripes are what make the face a sloth and not a bear. Outlining them doubles the lines around the eyes and turns to mud at 24 pt, so they are a 26 percent wash of the stroke colour instead, the one fill on the page.
  • Three claws, always. Otto is a three-toed sloth, the one with the smile and the mask. Claws are the second identifier after the mask and they survive at every size the figures appear.
  • Two stroke ratios, on purpose. The figures use 1.5 percent of their artboard, which lands on the mark's own weight when they sit at the sizes they meet (board 4). The badge uses 5 percent of its diameter because it lives at 24 to 56 pt, where the mark's ratio would be a hairline. Same as an optical size in a typeface.
  • No fur, no ears, no branch texture. The mark has none of those either. A finishing illustrator may add a few tufts on the crown of the large figures; nothing should be added to the badge.
  • What this is not. Not the App Store copy, not the Swift, not the final art. Design first, per the standing rule: the OttoMark placeholder becomes the badge only after the founders pick a direction here.
