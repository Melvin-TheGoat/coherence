One card holds the month; rows are separated by a hairline, which is the repo's own 14-app finding ("no boxes, bare text on a hairline") applied to the one screen that still had a box per item. The day is the headline because that is what somebody is looking for, the sound and the time sit under it, and the length is the only number on the right. A score chip appears only on a session that HAS one, so a phone sit is short a chip rather than short an explanation.

The length becomes the object, in an amber puck where the sloth used to be. It reads well and it spends the app's one gold-per-section rule seven times down a page, so nothing on the screen is emphasised any more. It also makes length look like the thing being graded, which is the exact reading "Your minutes" was written to avoid.

What is in a row, and what is not

FieldShownWhy
DayAlways, as the headline "Today", "Yesterday", "Friday", then "Sat, Aug 30" past a week. What a person scans for.
Sound or techniqueAlways, small, under the day The one thing that distinguishes two sits on the same day.
LengthAlways, right edge The honest measure of a phone sit, and the number the chart above is made of.
ScoreOnly when the session has one A wrist sit scores; a phone sit does not. A blank chip would be the row apologising.
PhotoOnly when one was taken 34 x 40, portrait, because every selfie is portrait. Nothing takes its place when absent.
OttoNever He is the portrait at the top of the page and he is on Home. Repeating him nineteen times down the same screen makes him furniture.
Heart / Still / BreathNot in the list Three readings per row is a table, and the row's job is to get you to the session. They are on the results screen, one tap away.
Recommendation: A. It is the shape the repo already decided on for Save session and never applied here, it fits three times as many sessions on a screen, and it is the only one of the three that does not introduce a new visual object. B draws attention to missed days and C spends gold on every row.
The thing A gives up, stated. The photo gets small. A selfie taken after a sit is currently the biggest thing on its card, and in a list it is a 34pt thumbnail. That is the right trade for the log, which is read by scrolling, and the photo is still full size on the session's own screen. If it turns out people scroll the log to look at their photos rather than to find a session, the answer is a separate grid, not a bigger row.
