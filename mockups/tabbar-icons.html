Tab bar icons

Today's bar uses four stock SF Symbols (house, hand.raised, person.2, person.crop.circle) around the one gold plus. Melvin's read:

"Speaking of the icons, i actually dont like these, they seem bland/generic/AI generated. Im wondering if we can make some more creative ones that are more on theme for this app."

Three directions below, drawn as inline SVG at the size they would actually render (24 to 28pt), so the path data can be carried straight into SwiftUI Shapes later. In every direction the selected tab reads in ink brown, never gold (gold stays the plus's alone), and the shift from unselected to selected is either a fill swap or an added tint, the same grammar the app already uses for "chosen" versus "shown."

Clean, familiar, and exactly what a thousand other apps ship. Nothing here says 808, and a raised hand for "Block" reads as a generic permission icon before it reads as "Otto is holding your apps."

Direction 1

Meadow: playful, illustrated

Bold rounded shapes filled with personality: a sun behind the home hill, a leaf standing up like a raised palm, two sloth heads for Friends, Otto's own face for Profile. Unselected is line only; selected goes solid, and small "eye" dots flip from drawn-on to cut-out.

Gets right: the warmest, most immediately "808" of the three. The filled state reads at a glance because whole shapes go solid, not just a stroke thickening, and Otto's face is the single most ownable mark on the page.

Risks: two overlapping heads and a leaf both lean toward mascot art rather than system chrome. Worth a real-device check that they don't compete with the gold plus for the first glance.

Direction 2

Line: quiet, minimal

One weight, mostly open strokes, nothing louder than the current set. Three of the four icons grow out of the meadow's own five-petal flower: a closed bud for Block, a bloom framing a portrait for Profile, two blooms overlapping for Friends. Selected keeps the same line and adds a soft tint, the way a chosen row gets a wash rather than a shout.

Gets right: closest to today's weight and restraint, so it is the lowest-risk swap, and the flower motifs tie straight into the meadow without a single new colour or shape family entering the app.

Risks: the tint-only selected state is the subtlest of the three and deserves a squint test in daylight. Two small blooms for Friends may register as decoration before they register as "people."

Direction 3

Otto: built around the mascot

Every icon is a piece of Otto's own body: the branch he lives on for Home, his raised three-toed paw for Block, two of his silhouettes for Friends, his eye mask alone for Profile. Unselected outlines the shape; selected fills it solid, with claws and mask cut back out of the fill as negative space rather than drawn on top.

Gets right: only makes sense for this app. Nobody else's tab bar has a raised three-toed paw, and it does double duty as "stop" and "sloth" in one shape. Nothing here could be mistaken for a stock icon pack.

Risks: the branch and the mask both need a beat longer to parse the first time, before the mascot's shapes are familiar. This is the direction to prototype on an actual phone before trusting it, since it is the furthest from a glance-and-go glyph.

Recommendation

Pick

Direction 1, Meadow. It answers what Melvin actually said: today's set does not look generic because the shapes are wrong, it looks generic because nothing in it belongs only to 808. Meadow fixes that (a sun, a leaf, Otto's own face) while staying fast to read, since every icon is still one or two big familiar silhouettes rather than a scene to decode.

Worth stealing on the way there: Direction 3's raised paw is a stronger single mark than Meadow's leaf for Block specifically, the one tab whose job ("Otto is holding your apps") is hardest to say in a glyph. A mixed set, Meadow everywhere with the paw standing in for Block, is worth putting on a real phone next to the pure versions before anything is final.

Whichever direction moves forward, check it at true 26pt on-device next to the gold plus and in direct sun before it goes into MainTabBar.swift. Nothing in this page can tell you how it feels under a thumb.
