808, made friendly

The app is hard to look at because it is dark, thin and grey: hairlines, small grey labels, one gold accent doing every job. This proposes the opposite. Light warm paper, fat rounded shapes, a real character, and three colours that each mean exactly one thing. Nothing about what 808 measures changes.

The palette

The current colour grammar is load-bearing and it survives intact, it just gets warmer and gains one member. Today gold does two jobs (your score AND your streak), which is why the home screen has gold in four places and reads busy. Splitting the streak onto coral means each colour answers one question, and the "one gold thing per section" rule finally holds without effort.

Otto, as an actual character

He is already a sloth. What he is not is cute: today he is thin line art, one stroke weight, no fill, which reads as an icon rather than someone. A mascot people like has filled shapes, a face that carries an expression, and cheeks. Below is the same sloth rebuilt that way, in three states, and then three alternative animals so the choice is made on sight rather than on argument.

Recommended
Otto, settled. The default. Eyes closed, cheeks warm, arms hanging. This is him on the home screen and asleep in the corner of everything.
Otto, listening. Eyes open, same everything else. This is the only face he wears in the chat, because someone explaining your session should be looking at you.
Otto, pleased. Arms up, eyes squeezed shut, two honey sparks. He shows up on an award and on a session that went well, and nowhere else, so it keeps meaning something.

Or a different animal entirely

Three that fit a meditation app. Judge them on which one you would want on your home screen for a year.

Turtle. Carries his own quiet room on his back. The strongest second choice: the shell is a shape you can put a score inside.
Frog. The most instantly readable as meditating, and the most generic: half the yoga studios on earth already use one.
Capybara. The internet's own symbol for unbothered calm. Funniest, most shareable, hardest to draw small.

My pick is the sloth, and not out of sunk cost. Slowness is the one virtue 808 actually sells: the score rewards settling rather than striving, and a sloth is the only one of these four whose entire personality is that. He is also already named, already written into the chat's voice, and no meditation app of any size has a mascot at all, so the lane is empty.

Same information, same order, same data. The difference is entirely ground, weight and radius: cream instead of near-black, filled chips instead of outlined ones, 24px corners instead of 12, and a streak that is coral so the only honey thing on the screen is a score.

What this actually is, in code

Most of it is seven colour files and one font function, which is why it is worth doing. The repo already routes every colour through AppColor and every reusable piece through DesignKit, so the reskin happens in the places the rules already made us put things.

  1. The colorsets. New warm values for the existing tokens, plus two new ones (a coral for the streak, a paper for the ground). Light becomes the default theme; dark stays and gets the same warm treatment.
  2. One font entry point. AppFont switches to design: .rounded throughout. This single change does more for friendliness than anything else on the list.
  3. DesignKit. Radii 12 to 22, hairlines become soft bottom shadows, buttons gain the pressed edge, EvidenceRow and MonthCalendar get filled chips instead of outlines.
  4. OttoArt.swift. The path strings are replaced with the filled character above, and it gains a fill layer it does not have today. Same parser, same tests, same three sizes.
  5. Charts. Thicker rounded strokes, same domains, same rules. Every honesty rule about axes survives.

Two things I am deliberately not changing. The colour grammar keeps its meanings (a ring is a measured score, teal is your body, one accent object per section), because that is what lets someone read a screen in one sweep. And no copy changes: this is a skin, and mixing a visual rebuild with a rewrite makes it impossible to tell which one worked.
