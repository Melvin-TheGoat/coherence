# 808 brand and marketing brief, for generated carousels

Paste this whole file into the carousel repo's chat as the context block. It is
self-contained: nothing here requires reading the 808 codebase.

---

## 1. What the product is

**808** is an iPhone + Apple Watch app that **scores your meditation from your
own body**. You meditate however you like, wearing your Apple Watch. When you
finish, 808 shows what your body actually did and gives you one score out of
100.

- **Website:** meditate808.com
- **Parent company:** Lock Out Inc. (write it exactly like that: a space, and
  `Inc.`). Only needed where a slide, caption or footer names the publisher.
  The product is always called 808, never the company name.
- **Instagram / TikTok:** @808meditate
- **Status:** pre-launch, waitlist open.
- **Requires:** a paired Apple Watch to measure a session.

**The three signals it measures:** stillness (body motion), heart-rate settling
across the session, and breathing rate read from the small tilt of the wrist
when you deliberately slow your breath.

**The one-sentence positioning:** *the first app that scores your meditation
from your own body.* Everything else is a feature.

**Real feature list** (never invent others): practice score out of 100 after
every session, a written plain-English verdict, heart rate / stillness /
breathing graphs you can scrub with a finger, streaks, a practiced-days
calendar, full session history, 17 awards, a shareable session card, a
25-minute guided journey, brainwave-paced tones (delta/theta/alpha),
traditional tunings (432, 528, 852, 963 Hz), nature sounds (rain, ocean,
forest, campfire), a written how-to-meditate guide, and bring-your-own-audio
(play anything from any other app while the Watch measures).

## 2. Voice

Calm, plain, confident, specific. Never hypey, never mystical, never clinical.
The product's whole position is *proof*, so the copy must never out-claim the
instrument.

**Hard rules:**

1. **NO EM DASHES. EVER.** This is the single most important copy rule. An em
   dash is the clearest tell that text was machine-written, and for a product
   whose pitch is honesty, prose that reads as generated undercuts the claim
   before anyone reaches the substance. Restructure the sentence instead of
   swapping in another mark. Usually two sentences, sometimes a colon, often
   just delete it.
2. **Name the subject on every slide.** Each slide is met in isolation while
   someone thumbs past. A headline like "You're not bad at this" fails, because
   the reader has not been told at what.
3. **No invented numbers about the user or the business.** No user counts, no
   "join 50,000 meditators", no fabricated ratings or reviews. We are
   pre-launch and have none of it. The only number 808 is entitled to is one
   measured off a wrist.
4. **No health outcome claims.** 808 does not treat, cure, diagnose or prevent
   anything. It is a wellness app. Never claim it lowers blood pressure, cures
   anxiety, fixes sleep, or changes brain states.
5. **Never claim to read brainwaves, theta, HRV, or nervous-system state.** The
   app measures motion and averaged heart rate. Saying otherwise is a lie the
   product can be caught in.
6. **Never use the "23 minutes to refocus after an interruption" statistic.**
   It is the most-quoted focus stat on the internet and it has no paper behind
   it. It traces to a 2006 interview, and the study everyone cites found the
   opposite.
7. Spell out technical terms rather than using arrows or shorthand chains.

**Words that fit:** measured, evidence, proof, settled, still, landed, honest,
after, wrist, score, showed up.
**Words to avoid:** journey (as a synonym for life), unlock your potential,
transform, hack, optimize, biohack, rewire your brain, scientifically proven.

## 3. Colour

The app and the website share one palette. **One accent only: gold.** There is
no secondary accent colour in marketing.

| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Background | `#13100D` | The ground of every slide. Near-black, warm. |
| Card | `#221E19` | Panels, quote boxes, stat tiles. |
| Border | `#2E2A23` | Hairlines, panel edges. |
| Foreground | `#F5F3EC` | Headlines and primary text. Warm off-white, never pure white. |
| Muted | `#9A9A93` | Body copy, captions, secondary lines. |
| Dim | `#55534E` | Disabled, de-emphasised, slide numbers. |
| **Gold** | **`#D4AF37`** | The one accent. Highlighted words, the mark, the CTA, one number per slide. |
| Gold ink | `#17130A` | Text sitting ON a gold fill (buttons). |

**Colour rules:**

- **Every slide is dark.** Warm near-black ground, light text. Never a white or
  light slide; the app is dark and a white carousel promises a screen that does
  not exist.
- **Exactly ONE gold thing per slide.** A gold headline word, OR a gold number,
  OR a gold button. Two gold objects on one slide means neither is the point.
  This rule is enforced everywhere in the product and it is what makes the
  brand look deliberate.
- Gold at 8 to 12% opacity over the background makes the standard highlight
  panel; gold at 25 to 40% makes its border.
- Never use a gradient background other than a very subtle warm radial glow
  behind a headline (gold at ~14% opacity, heavily blurred, top third).

## 4. Typography

Three faces, all free on Google Fonts, all already used by the website.

| Role | Family | Weight | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Display / headlines** | **Manrope** | 700 to 800 | Tight tracking, about `-0.02em`. Sentence case. Text-wrap balanced. |
| **Body** | **Hanken Grotesk** | 400, 600 for emphasis | Line height ~1.55. |
| **Labels / kickers / numbers-as-labels** | **DM Mono** | 400 to 500 | UPPERCASE, letter-spacing `0.08em` to `0.1em`, ~12px equivalent. Always gold or muted. |

Fallback stack if a face is unavailable: `ui-sans-serif, system-ui, sans-serif`
for Manrope and Hanken; `ui-monospace, SFMono-Regular, Menlo, monospace` for DM
Mono.

**Typographic conventions:**

- Every section or slide group opens with a **DM Mono uppercase kicker** (e.g.
  `01 / THE PROBLEM`), gold, small.
- Big statistics are **Manrope 800**, very large, with the mono kicker above
  them naming the felt effect, not the metric. ("A sharper mind" above
  "+16 points", never the reverse.)
- Headlines are short. Two lines maximum on a carousel slide.
- Never centre long body copy. Headlines may centre; paragraphs left-align.

## 5. The mark

The 808 logo is a gold quatrefoil: four thin ellipses crossing at the centre
(two vertical, two horizontal) with a small circle in the middle, stroked, not
filled. It reads as a stylised flower or an atom.

Use the app icon file as the source asset rather than redrawing it. If the mark
is unavailable, set the wordmark **808** in Manrope 800 in gold and use that
alone. Do not invent a new symbol.

Slide-corner branding: the mark at small size plus `808` in Manrope 800 gold,
or just `meditate808.com` in DM Mono uppercase muted. One or the other, bottom
of the final slide.

## 6. Carousel format and structure

- **Canvas:** 1080 x 1350 (4:5 portrait) for Instagram carousels. 1080 x 1080
  if a square set is requested. Reels/TikTok stills: 1080 x 1920.
- **Safe margins:** keep all text at least 90px from every edge; Instagram
  crops the sides in some views.
- **Slide count:** 6 to 8. Slide 1 is the hook, the last slide is the CTA.
- **One idea per slide.** If a slide needs a second sentence to survive, the
  idea is too big.
- **Slide 1 must work with the sound off and no context.** It is the only slide
  most people see.

**Default structure (problem-first, which is how the website argues):**

1. Hook: name the pain as the reader would say it.
2. Sharpen it: the specific, familiar version of that pain.
3. The turn: what has been missing is evidence, not effort.
4. The mechanism: what 808 measures, in plain words.
5. Show it: a real screenshot (score, or the graphs).
6. What it changes: the habit, the streak, the proof.
7. CTA: waitlist at meditate808.com, follow @808meditate.

---

## 7. Awareness stages, and which hook to use

Apply Eugene Schwartz's *Breakthrough Advertising*: channel existing desire,
never manufacture it, and meet the reader where they already are.

| Reader | What they need | Hook style |
|---|---|---|
| **Never meditated** | The problem named, and permission | "You are not bad at meditating. You were never told whether it was working." |
| **Tried and quit** | The mechanism: why THIS works when the last app did not | "Every meditation app asks you to trust it. This one shows you the reading." |
| **Meditates daily** | Only the new claim | "Your meditation, scored out of 100, measured on your wrist." |

**Intensify by demonstration, not adjectives.** Show the low-scoring session
next to the high one. Show the real graph. The product's position is proof, so
a screenshot always beats a claim.

## 9. Research you MAY cite (and exactly how)

These are the only numbers approved for marketing. Cite them as being about
**meditation as a practice**, never as evidence that 808 works. Nobody has run
that trial.

- **46.9%** of waking moments are spent thinking about something other than
  what you are doing. Killingsworth & Gilbert 2010, *Science*,
  doi:10.1126/science.1192439. 2,250 adults.
- **+16 percentile points** on GRE reading comprehension after two weeks of
  meditation training, randomized. Mrazek et al. 2013, *Psychological Science*,
  doi:10.1177/0956797612459659.
- **3,515 participants across 47 randomized trials** showed real reductions in
  anxiety and depression. Goyal et al. 2014, *JAMA Internal Medicine*,
  doi:10.1001/jamainternmed.2013.13018. Effect sizes around 0.3, so describe it
  as real and modest, never dramatic.
- The **American Heart Association** says meditation may be considered as an
  addition to standard care, never a replacement. Levine et al. 2017,
  doi:10.1161/JAHA.117.002218. Their verdict is deliberately modest; keep ours
  the same.

**Carry the caveat.** Any slide citing research needs a closing line to the
effect of: this is the research on meditation, not on 808, and none of it is
medical advice. The website does this and it is part of why the argument lands.

**Do NOT use the 48%-fewer-cardiac-events figure in social content.** It exists
on the website with three paragraphs of context (the trial was run by the group
that teaches the technique, an earlier version was withdrawn, and the
population was already ill). A carousel cannot carry that context, and the
number without it is misleading.

## 10. Testimonials you may quote

Real, verbatim, from beta testers. Keep the typos, use first names only, and
never label them as anything grander than early testers.

- "I've started and quit meditating probably six times. The thing that always
  got me was having no idea if anything was happening. First session with this
  showed my heart rate dropped 14 beats when i slowed my breathing down and it
  showed me where on the graph."
- "Big one for me is I already have meditations I like on YouTube. This just
  runs in the background on my watch and measures. Don't have to switch to
  their library or listen to some voice I don't like."
- "Skeptical it could pick up breathing from a wrist but it caught me slowing
  down at the start of a session and then speeding up later on, surprisingly
  accurate"
- "I meditate a lot and always wanted to have a way of sharing that I do, now I
  send my friend my meditation score everytime"
- "I track everything else so figured why not my morning meditation as well. I
  love the streak, keeps me accountable, and the achievements are also fun to
  hit"
- "Complete beginner to meditating and tried it out with low expectations, the
  guide turned out to be incredibly helpful in explaining the purpose and
  technique to meditating."

Do not write new testimonials. Do not paraphrase these into cleaner English.

## 12. Calls to action

Pre-launch, the only real CTA is the waitlist.

- Primary: **Join the waitlist at meditate808.com**
- Secondary: **Follow @808meditate** (Instagram and TikTok; there is no X
  account, do not invent one)
- Never write "download now" or "get it on the App Store" until launch.

Caption style: two or three short sentences in the same voice as the slides,
then the CTA, then hashtags on their own line. No emoji walls. One emoji at
most, and usually none.

## 13. Quick self-check before publishing any set

1. Any em dashes? Remove them by restructuring.
2. Does slide 1 make sense to a stranger with no context?
3. Is there exactly one gold element per slide?
4. Any number about users, downloads or ratings that we did not measure?
5. Any health claim, brainwave claim, or "scientifically proven"?
6. Does any research slide carry the "this is about meditation, not about 808"
   caveat?
7. Is every screenshot a real one?
