04 / Meditation works

Meditation itself has the evidence.

+16

47

Meditation trials showing real reductions in anxiety and depression.3

AHA

05 / The catch

Almost nobody keeps meditating.

39%

Dropped out across 70 trials of meditation apps, and researchers were often paying them to continue.6

~half

Of paying Calm subscribers stopped using the app, out of 3,275 tracked in the real world.7

280,000

Meditation sessions analysed. How often you meditate predicted improvement. How long each one lasted showed no significant effect.8

Coming back is the part that matters, and coming back is the part with nothing to show for it. So we built the part with something to show.

06 / What 808 does about it

Four problems, four answers.

Pain 1 / “Am I even doing this right?”

Every session scored, honestly.

Heart rate, stillness and breathing, drawn as curves you can read. One score out of 100. A written takeaway in plain words.

And when a session goes badly, 808 says so. A restless sit scores a 24, not a participation trophy. That is exactly why the good sessions mean something.

Pain 2 / “Nothing keeps me accountable”

A streak that notices, and awards you keep.

Your streak, a calendar of practiced days, and seventeen awards earned by things you actually did. An award never expires: break a streak and what you earned stays earned.

And you will not be doing it alone. Group meditations and challenges run on @808meditate.

Pain 3 / “I can't prove I did it”

Share the proof.

Every session becomes a card carrying your measured score, your curves and your streak. Post it to Instagram, X, or anywhere else. Not a claim that you meditated. A reading.

Pain 4 / “I don't know how”

From first sit to advanced practice.

A built-in guide with eight methods, easiest first. Complete beginners start with what meditation even is, then counting down and the body scan. Further along: dual awareness, blessing the energy centers, manifestation.

Or ignore all of it. Play a guided meditation or frequency track from YouTube or Spotify, or sit in silence. The Watch measures either way.

Where these numbers come from
  1. Killingsworth, M. A., & Gilbert, D. T. (2010). A wandering mind is an unhappy mind. Science, 330(6006), 932. doi:10.1126/science.1192439. 2,250 adults, experience sampling. Mind wandering in 46.9% of samples. Time-lag analysis supported wandering causing unhappiness rather than the reverse.
  2. Mrazek, M. D., Franklin, M. S., Phillips, D. T., Baird, B., & Schooler, J. W. (2013). Mindfulness training improves working memory capacity and GRE performance while reducing mind wandering. Psychological Science, 24(5), 776–781. doi:10.1177/0956797612459659.
  3. Goyal, M., et al. (2014). Meditation programs for psychological stress and well-being. JAMA Internal Medicine, 174(3), 357–368. doi:10.1001/jamainternmed.2013.13018. 47 trials with active controls, 3,515 participants. Moderate evidence, effect sizes around 0.3.
  4. Levine, G. N., et al. (2017). Meditation and cardiovascular risk reduction: a scientific statement from the American Heart Association. Journal of the American Heart Association, 6(10), e002218. doi:10.1161/JAHA.117.002218. A systematic review by the AHA's own councils. Its verdict is deliberately modest: meditation may be considered as an adjunct to guideline-directed care, never as a replacement for it, and the overall quality and quantity of the data are described as modest.
  5. Schneider, R. H., et al. (2012). Stress reduction in the secondary prevention of cardiovascular disease. Circulation: Cardiovascular Quality and Outcomes, 5(6), 750–758. doi:10.1161/CIRCOUTCOMES.112.967406. 201 Black adults with existing coronary heart disease, randomized to Transcendental Meditation or health education, followed a median 5.4 years. 48% relative reduction in the composite of death, myocardial infarction and stroke. Two things you should know: the trial was run by the Maharishi University institute that teaches the technique, and an earlier version of the paper was withdrawn by Archives of Internal Medicine in 2011, then reanalyzed and published here. It studied one technique in one population that was already ill, so it says nothing about what meditation does for a healthy person.
  6. Linardon, J., et al. (2023). Rates of attrition and engagement in randomized controlled trials of mindfulness apps. Behaviour Research and Therapy, 170, 104421. doi:10.1016/j.brat.2023.104421. 70 trials, 9,258 participants. 24.7% pooled attrition, 38.7% in larger trials.
  7. Mindfulness meditation app abandonment during the COVID-19 pandemic: an observational study. Mindfulness (2023). doi:10.1007/s12671-023-02125-4. 3,275 Calm subscribers; 47.9% to 53.2% abandonment depending on cohort.
  8. Cearns, M., & Clark, S. R. (2023). The effects of dose, practice habits, and objects of focus on digital meditation effectiveness and adherence. Journal of Medical Internet Research, 25, e43358. doi:10.2196/43358. 280,000 sessions from 10,409 people. Consistency rather than length best predicted improvement; session length showed no significant association with mood (p=.19).

What these do and do not show. The research above establishes that mind wandering is costly, that meditation training helps, that consistency beats session length, and that people quit apps. It does not show that 808 specifically will do any of this for you, and nothing here is medical advice or a reason to change anything your doctor told you. Nobody has run that trial yet, and we won't pretend otherwise.
